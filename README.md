# Lumen

A private AI tutor that runs inside your web browser. The AI model (Qwen3.5) runs on your own computer's graphics chip with the WebLLM engine. Nothing you type is sent to an AI company.

## What you need

- **Google Chrome 113 or newer** (best), or **Safari on macOS 26 (Tahoe)** or newer.
- About **1.1 GB** of free space for the recommended model (Qwen3.5 2B). The 4B model needs 2.4 GB, the 0.8B model 0.45 GB, and the new 9B model (for strong computers only, about 16 GB of RAM) 5.1 GB.
- Internet the first time (to download the model). After that the AI works offline. Look-ups, weather and news need the internet.

## Put it online with GitHub Pages (recommended)

This gives you a normal link. You download nothing yourself; the browser saves the model the first time you open the page.

1. On github.com, click **New repository**. Name it `lumen`, set it to **Public**, and click **Create repository**.
2. On the new repository page, click **uploading an existing file**.
3. Drag in all the files from this folder: `index.html`, `sw.js`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `icon.svg`, `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`, `core-facts.txt`, `LICENSE`, `NOTICE`, `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, and the `licenses` folder and the `vendor` folder (the libraries and the AI engine; since version 16 the page needs them). Click **Commit changes**. (The `tools` folder is for your own Mac, to make the offline Wikipedia file, and the `tests` folder and `package.json` are for checking Lumen on your computer. They are fine in the repository, but the page does not need them.)
4. Go to **Settings → Pages**. Under "Branch", choose **main** and **/ (root)**, then click **Save**.
5. Wait one or two minutes. Your link is `https://<your-username>.github.io/lumen/`.
6. Open the link in Chrome and click **Download and start**.

If you already put an older Lumen online: upload the new files over the old ones, open the link, and press **Cmd+Shift+R** once to load the new version.

Tip: in Chrome you can click the install icon in the address bar to add Lumen to your Dock like an app.

## Try it without GitHub

Since version 16, opening `index.html` by double-click (as a file) no longer starts the AI: Chrome does not let a page that was opened as a file load the engine file from the `vendor` folder, and Lumen then says so. Instead, open the Terminal, go to the Lumen folder (it must contain `index.html` and `vendor`), run `python3 -m http.server 8000`, and open `http://localhost:8000` in **Google Chrome**. Nothing leaves your computer for this. The GitHub Pages link is easier.

## It keeps learning

A web page cannot retrain the AI model itself (that needs a big computer and a lot of time). So Lumen learns the way a good assistant does: it keeps notes on your computer and shows the right ones to the model with every question. All of it is saved in your browser only. You can see and control everything in **Settings → Memory → Learning**, or type `/learned`.

- **It remembers what you tell it.** Say "I have an iPhone 17" and it is saved, with a small "Remembered" label under your message and an Undo button. Say "I now use an iPhone 18" later and the new fact replaces the old one. Say "Jimmy Donaldson is Mr. Beast" and Lumen knows both names mean the same thing, and even searches for the other name too. Other things it picks up: your name, age, where you live, your job or school, things you own, pets, "my exam is on Friday", things you like, and "from now on, answer in short sentences". It ignores questions, guesses and "what if" sentences, and it never saves health, religion, money or similar topics by itself (type "remember …" yourself if you want those saved). Say "forget my iPhone" to remove a fact.
- **It understands which one you mean.** Many names belong to more than one thing. If you type "mr beast", Lumen looks at which page people actually read the most (Wikipedia's page views of the last 60 days), so you get the YouTuber and not a little-known album. It also searches the other spelling ("mrbeast"). Under the answer it says which one it picked and shows a button for each other meaning ("Not what you meant?"). Tap one and Lumen answers again about that meaning and **remembers your choice** for that name, with an Undo button. If you write the kind of thing ("mr beast album"), it follows your words. You can see and forget your choices in `/learned`.
- **It notices what you like.** It keeps count of the topics you ask about. If you often tap "Simpler" it starts answering in simpler words by itself; the same for short answers, detailed answers and examples. Your favorite topics show up as a suggestion on the start screen.
- **It comes with built-in facts.** About 310 short facts are packed inside Lumen itself: who leads the big countries and organisations (checked on 11 October 2026), science, maths, history, geography, health, money, technology, sports results, big events of 2025–2026 (for example the 2026 World Cup, the 2026 Nobel prizes and the Iran war), everyday facts and a part about Slovenia. For every question Lumen searches them on your computer and gives the ones that fit to the AI, with the label "Built-in core facts" under the answer. They work without internet and are never sent anywhere. Turn them off in Settings → Memory → Learning → "Use the built-in facts". They do not update by themselves: they are as new as the version of Lumen you have (the page tells the AI how old they are, and after 60 days it warns the user that they may be out of date).
- **It says how far to trust an answer.** Under factual answers there is a small label, "Confidence: High", "Medium" or "Low". Tap it to see why. It is decided by evidence, not by the AI's own feeling: High means sources (or exact code results, or your file) were in front of the AI; Low means nothing was found to check it against and the answer states specific dates, numbers or names. It does not check each sentence yet, so even "High" means "has sources", not "proven". Poems, small talk and code get no label.
- **Check facts.** Under a factual answer, the **Check facts** button lists up to 5 claims from the answer (dates, names and numbers first), looks each one up in the built-in facts and in Wikipedia, and marks it **Supported**, **Could not confirm** or **Contradicted**, with the source. "Could not confirm" does not mean wrong. A contradiction turns the confidence label to Low. The AI model judges each passage, and a small model can misjudge, so check important things yourself. The results are saved with the answer.
- **Both sides.** For debate questions ("Should schools ban phones?", "Is it fair to ...") Lumen answers in a fixed layout: the question in neutral words, the best case for each side, what is disputed, what to check next, and a counter-argument idea for your essay. It gives no opinion of its own. Code checks that the two sides are about the same length (within 20%) and warns you if one side is much longer. You can also type `/both your question`. "X better than Y" product questions still get the normal comparison table.
- **Battery saver.** Settings → Model → Battery saver. When on: shorter answers, a smaller memory, and no background AI work (no search-word planning, no chat summaries). A badge at the top shows while it is on. It turns on by itself under 30% battery when not charging (Chrome can report the battery, Safari and Firefox cannot) and tells you when it does. If answers become more than 40% slower than at the start of your session, Lumen shows a tip that the laptop is hot.
- **Daily review.** Under flashcards (`/cards`), press **Save to daily review**. The cards then come back after 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 days (five boxes): **Got it** moves a card up one box, **Missed** sends it back to box 1, **Not sure** keeps it where it is. The home screen says "12 cards due today", and `/review` opens a round of up to 20 cards. A streak counts the days in a row you reviewed. Days use your computer's own local time. Lumen only knows what is due when it is open: it sends no reminders. Cards are saved in this browser (up to 400) and are part of the backup.
- **Essay feedback.** Type `/essay`, paste your essay and, if you have it, your teacher's rubric. Lumen reads one paragraph at a time and gives: the top 3 fixes, then Structure, Clarity, Grammar, Evidence and Style, and (with a rubric) an estimated level per criterion. Every comment quotes your exact sentence, and code checks that the quote really is in your essay; comments with an invented quote are dropped and the answer says how many. Only one or two short example rewrites are shown, never a rewritten essay. It reads up to 3,000 words, about 14 paragraphs.
- **Show-your-work solver.** For school math and science problems, type `/solve your problem` (a plain "Solve 3x + 7 = 22" gets the same layout by itself). The answer always has six parts: **Given**, **Find**, **Method**, **Steps**, **Check** and **Answer**. If the AI leaves some parts out, Lumen asks it once to write the whole solution again (it keeps the better version and says so). Then code recomputes every simple step written like `22 - 7 = 15` or `3 × 4 = 12`. If the AI's sum is wrong, a yellow note shows the sum and the calculator's result; if all are right, a quiet note says so. `/solve` also skips the Wikipedia look-up, because a math problem does not need one.
- **Roles.** The tools button (the sliders) in the message box, under **Role** (or `/role tutor`, `/role writing`, `/role business`, `/role coding`, `/role none`) gives the chat a working style: **Tutor** (explains in steps, then asks you one check question and does not give its answer), **Writing coach** (top 3 fixes first, quotes your words, no full rewrites), **Business helper** (summary, numbers, next steps, says which numbers are assumptions) and **Coding helper** (code first, then a plain explanation). The role belongs to the chat, is saved with it, and changes the starter buttons on the home screen. It adds about 150 words to every message the AI reads, which uses a little more memory and time per answer.
- **It saves what it looks up.** Every Wikipedia article it reads for you is kept as a short note. The next question about it is faster, and it still works when you have no internet (the answer says the notes are older).
- **It reads a little of the internet every day, and gets a bit smarter each time.** About once a day, while Lumen is open, it reads Wikipedia's news, today's featured article and the 8 most-read articles. For your favorite topics (up to 5) it saves two articles each and headlines, and for your number-one topic it does a longer read, so a later question gets more than the opening lines. It also **tries again for things it could not find**: if you asked about something and nothing was found, Lumen keeps the words for up to 14 days and looks again in the daily reading (articles and news appear over time). And it **refreshes older notes** that it used before (up to 5 a day), so they do not go stale. Press **Learn now** to do it right away.

Every switch can be turned off, and "Forget what Lumen noticed" and "Empty the saved notes" erase what it learned. Facts about you (memory) can hold 60 items, and Lumen remembers up to 60 meanings you chose. The backup file includes your memory, your chosen meanings and saved notes.

## What is new in version 18: a new look, a setup for each computer, and a fixed 9B download

**The 9B download loop is fixed.** The problem: after a long download Lumen said a file was broken, deleted the whole model, downloaded all 5.1 GB again, and could do that over and over (also every time the page opened, because the model still looked "saved"). Now Lumen downloads the model files itself and checks every one:

- **Every file is checked** against the size the model's own index says it must have, before and after it is saved. A browser that hands back a damaged copy is noticed at once.
- **Only a broken file is downloaded again**, never the whole model. Settings → Model → **Check the saved files** checks them by hand and repairs only what is broken.
- **Big files come in 32 MB pieces**, 4 files at a time, each with up to 6 tries. If the connection drops, only the piece that was cut is fetched again. When the internet comes back, the download carries on by itself. **Pause download** keeps the finished files.
- **Free space is checked before the download starts**, and the browser is asked to keep the files, so you do not wait an hour to learn that there is no room.
- **A browser that keeps damaging files** (in its Cache API) is switched to its private file storage (OPFS) for that model, by itself.
- **No more loops:** at most two repairs per start and four per day. A model that stopped the page twice while loading (too big for the computer) is not started by itself any more; Lumen offers the next smaller one in one click. The old index file that WebLLM's own "delete" left behind (and that could never be repaired) is now removed too.
- Choosing another model during a download stops that download (its finished files are kept) and starts the new one at once.

**A setup for each computer.** The first time you open Lumen in a browser it asks a few questions: which computer this is (MacBook Air, MacBook Pro, iMac or Mac mini, Windows laptop or PC, Chromebook, tablet, phone), how much memory it has (8, 16, 24, 32 GB), which chip or graphics card, what you use Lumen for, how answers should sound, whether to look things up, and light or dark. Then it recommends the model that fits (for example 2B for an 8 GB MacBook Air, 4B for 16 GB, 9B for a 16 GB MacBook Pro with a Pro chip or anything with 24 GB or more), checks the free space and downloads it by itself. Each computer keeps its own answers, so your MacBook Air and your MacBook Pro each get their own model. Do it again from the profile menu (bottom left), the model button, or Settings → Model.

**A new look, in the style of Claude.** Warm cream colours with a terracotta accent, a serif greeting and serif answers, and a quiet sidebar: **New chat**, **Search**, **Projects**, **Artifacts**, **Starred** and recent chats, and your profile at the bottom. The message box is one rounded card with **+** (files, quizzes, templates), the **tools** button (look-ups, answer style, role), the thinking button, the **model picker** and the send button. Settings that are not the usual ones show as small tags you can remove. Dark mode matches. Lumen keeps its own name and mark; it is not made by or connected to Anthropic.

**New things, like the big AI apps have:**

- **Projects** (like Claude and ChatGPT Projects): instructions and files that every chat in the project uses, for example one project per school subject.
- **Artifacts and a side panel** (like Claude's Artifacts and ChatGPT's Canvas): press **Open** on a code block to see a web page, picture or diagram next to the chat, switch to the code, edit it, copy it or save it. **Artifacts** in the sidebar lists everything from all chats. A long answer can be opened as a document to read, edit and save.
- **Temporary chats** (like Claude's incognito chat): not saved, not in the list, and Lumen learns nothing from them.
- **Voice conversation** (like ChatGPT's voice mode): speak, Lumen answers out loud and listens again. It uses dictation (Settings → Voice), with the same privacy rules.
- **Your own answer styles** (like Claude's styles), plus Explanatory and Formal.
- **Change an answer** (like Gemini): Shorter, Longer, More casual, More formal. **Branch in a new chat** (like ChatGPT). **Continue** when an answer hit the length limit. **Select words in an answer** to quote them in your reply. **Star** chats. Times on your messages. Serif or sans answers (Settings → General → Appearance).
- The start screen has Claude-style categories (Learn, Write, Code, Look things up, Life stuff) with ready prompts; the ones you use come first.

**Fixed glitches** found by the new tests: "5 foots" (now "5 feet", "10 inches", "metres per second"); "what day is 25 December 2026?" answered with today's date; a short one-page PDF was refused as "a scan"; buttons on the download card could miss a click while the progress moved; backups now include projects and styles and never temporary chats.

**Tests.** The folder `tests/` has 57 end-to-end tests (`node tests/run.mjs`, see `tests/README.md`). They drive the real page in Chromium with a pretend AI engine that reads and checks model files exactly the way WebLLM does, and pretend versions of Hugging Face and Wikipedia, so they need no graphics chip and no internet.

## What is new in version 17: a restart guard and a stronger model

**The page that "reset by itself".** A page can stop without any error message: the browser closes a tab that uses a lot of graphics memory, puts it to sleep, or the graphics chip freezes. The page then starts again and the model looks "gone". This could not be reproduced without the real computer, so version 17 does four things that work for every cause:

- **A restart note.** While the model loads or an answer is written, Lumen keeps a tiny note in this tab's session storage (model, time, whether the tab is in front). A normal close removes it. If it is still there at the next start, Lumen says what probably happened (tab put to sleep, tab in the background, or a crash, most likely graphics memory) and turns the unfinished answer into a message with a "Try again" button. After two crashes with the same model in one day it lowers the memory size by one step by itself and says so.
- **The screen stays awake while the AI works** (Settings → Model → This computer; on by default; uses the Screen Wake Lock, which needs no permission). A sleeping screen can make the browser stop the graphics chip.
- **A watchdog.** If no word arrives for 5 minutes at the start, or 2 minutes later on, Lumen stops the frozen engine (it no longer waits for it forever), loads the saved model again by itself, and shows "Try again". The same happens when the browser reports a lost graphics device. Time when the computer slept is not counted.
- **A clear note if the saved model is really gone.** If the model loaded here before but the browser no longer has the files (browsers delete site data when the disk is almost full), Lumen says so instead of silently showing "Download". Settings also shows how many restarts happened in 24 hours and whether the browser promised to keep the files.

**A stronger model: Qwen3.5 9B** (Apache 2.0, free, no sign-in). About 5.1 GB to download and 6.4 GB of graphics memory; made for 16 GB computers (for example a Mac with an M-series Pro chip). The browser cannot see more than 8 GB of RAM, so Lumen asks once before it is chosen. Under a weak answer, Lumen offers it after the 4B model, but only if you said your computer is strong.

**Max thinking mode (9B only).** Plans the searches, reads up to 6 sources and 36,000 characters of files, thinks up to 3,000 tokens and can write up to 2,200. Choose it in the thinking menu, with `/max question`, or "Think the hardest (Max)" under an answer. It is the slowest and most careful mode. With any other model, Max quietly becomes Deep.

## What is new in version 16: the legal report

A legal checklist for an EU/Slovenian hobby project (12 October 2026) was compared with Lumen, point by point. Most points were already done in version 13 (AI notice, privacy, terms, credits, helpline, "Delete all my data", labels on copied and saved text). These were the gaps, now fixed:

- **No content network any more.** The report says to bundle the libraries instead of loading them from a public CDN. marked, DOMPurify, KaTeX (with its fonts), highlight.js, PDF.js, Mermaid and the WebLLM engine are now unchanged copies in the folder `vendor/` (`vendor/README.md` says where each file comes from and how to update it). jsDelivr no longer sees anyone's internet address, and the privacy page and Settings say so. A saved chat page asks for the maths style only when the chat has maths, and from this site.
- **Licences for what is now shipped.** Each `vendor/` folder has its library's licence. Mermaid and WebLLM are bundles with smaller libraries inside, so each has a `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.txt`.
- **Fonts.** The two fonts built into the page (Figtree and Fraunces, SIL Open Font License 1.1) had no notice. Their licence texts are in `licenses/`, and they are named in `NOTICE`, `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` and the Credits page.
- **Two safety lines for the AI.** Say that you have no reliable information about private people (anyone who is not a well-known public figure), and give general information only for health, law, money and safety, with a pointer to a doctor, lawyer or other qualified person. About 70 tokens more in every prompt.
- **Data note.** `NOTICE` now says what is in `core-facts.txt` (short facts in our own words, no copied Wikipedia text) and that an offline Wikipedia pack you build is CC BY-SA 4.0 and stays so if you share it.
- **Screen readers.** The chat is a labelled log that does not read out every word while it is written, and a hidden status line says "Lumen is working on an answer." and "Lumen finished the answer."
- **Service worker.** The five small libraries are saved for offline use when the page is first installed, the others when first used. If a file is missing offline, the browser now gets an error for that file instead of the page.
- **Cost.** Repository: about 11 MB more (engine 6.5 MB, Mermaid 2.6 MB, PDF reader 1.4 MB, the rest 1 MB). At page start the browser fetches only about 0.5 MB of it. The service worker keeps what was used (up to about 11 MB). Time: none noticeable; the files come from the same site instead of another one.
- **One thing that is different.** A page opened by double-click (as a file) can no longer start the AI, because Chrome blocks the engine file there. Lumen now says so in plain words and shows the two-line way to start a small local server. The web link works as before.
- **Not done on purpose.** A strict Content-Security-Policy: the AI engine runs WebAssembly and workers, and I cannot test the real engine here, so a wrong policy could stop it silently. Renaming "Lumen" (the report says it is fine for a free hobby project). A full postal address (the report says to ask a lawyer).

## What is new in version 15: fewer wrong answers, and a way to see it get better

Why: a small AI model sometimes writes a year, a number or a name that is not in the notes it was given. Before this version, nothing checked that. Now something does, you can tell Lumen when it is wrong, and the Usage page shows whether it is getting better.

- **Source check (new, on by default).** When an answer used notes or your files, plain code (not the AI) looks at every year, every big number or percent, and every person-like name in the answer and looks for it in everything the AI was given: the notes, your files, the exact results and your own words. If something is in none of them, the AI is asked **once** to write the answer again without it. The new answer is used only if it has fewer such details. A notice above the answer says what was found and taken out. Anything that is left is listed as "Not found in the sources", and the confidence label goes down. When everything is found, the confidence text says so (and says it did not check the meaning of each sentence). Switch it off in Settings → General → Answers → **Check answers against sources**.
- **Steadier answers when sources exist.** When an answer rests on notes or files, "Balanced" creativity works like "Precise" (less randomness, fewer made-up details). The last line the AI reads before it writes is now: take every date, number and name from the notes, and leave a detail out if it is not there. If you chose "Creative", that stays.
- **👍 and 👎 under every answer.** 👍 counts the answer as good and, when it had sources and a clean source check, keeps it, so the same question gets it as a note next time. 👎 opens a small form: what went wrong, and (optional) what the right answer is. The right answer is saved as something **you taught Lumen** (it shows in Settings → Memory, where you can delete it), the topic goes on the list for the daily reading, and a button offers "Answer again". Press a thumb again to take it back.
- **Answer quality (Usage page).** How many answers were checked, how many had every date, number and name in the sources, how many Lumen fixed, how many you marked good or wrong, how many corrections you taught, a comparison of the last 7 days with the 7 days before, a "Checked OK" line in the 14-day chart and table, and what Lumen has learned so far (saved notes, liked answers, corrections, chosen meanings, topics to read about).
- **A bigger model, when it matters.** When an answer is rated Low, has details that no source contains, or you marked it wrong, Lumen offers "Answer again with Qwen3.5 4B" (or 2B when you use 0.8B), but only if the computer reports enough memory. If the model is not saved yet, it asks first and says the download size.
- **Time, memory and disk.** The source check itself takes a few milliseconds. The rewrite takes about as long as one more short answer, and happens only when something was found (battery saver never does it). Ratings and counts add a few bytes a day. Liked answers are short saved notes (up to 150, about 1 KB each). Each correction is one of your 60 memory facts. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **How does Lumen "get better every day"?** The AI model itself does not change inside a web page. What gets better is what it is given: the daily reading, your corrections and good answers, and the source check. The Usage page shows the result in numbers, so you do not have to guess.

## What is new in version 14: simpler Settings and a Usage page

- **Settings is now a set of pages.** A menu on the left (on a phone: a list, then the page, with a back arrow) with nine pages: **General** (profile, looks, answers), **Model** (the AI model, working memory, battery saver, this computer), **Memory** (what Lumen remembers, your rules, learning), **Look-ups** (the sources, web search, offline Wikipedia), **Voice** (read aloud, dictation), **Usage**, **Data and privacy** (your data, backups, what leaves this computer), **Advanced** (base instructions, example answers, three tries, Test lab, shortcuts) and **About** (how Lumen works, credits). Everything that was in Settings is still there, just sorted. Long explanations now sit behind a small **More** button, and each look-up source is one short row with a switch and a **Details** button.
- **Usage page.** Tokens used (read and written), answers, screen time and speed (tokens per second), for today, 7 days, 30 days or all time; a 14-day chart (tokens, answers or minutes) that can also be shown as a table; where the tokens went (by model and by thinking mode, and how many the Test lab used); this visit; days in a row; **Copy summary**; **Reset usage numbers**. A token is a small piece of a word (about 4 tokens make 3 words). Screen time only counts while Lumen is open, visible and in use (a click or key within the last 90 seconds, or an answer being written).
- **Break reminder** (off by default). After 45 minutes of use without a 5-minute pause, a friendly message suggests a break.
- **New choices in General.** **Text size** (small, normal, large; answers and your messages), **Send with** (Enter, or Ctrl/⌘ + Enter so that Enter makes a new line), and **Thinking**, **Style** and **Creativity** all in one place.
- **Memory, disk and privacy.** The usage numbers are one small line per day in your browser (about 6 KB for a month of daily use, and at most about 80 KB, because only the last 400 days are kept). They hold only numbers, no text from your chats. They are never sent anywhere. **Delete all my data** and **Reset usage numbers** remove them. When the AI engine does not report token numbers, Lumen estimates them from the text length. The tiny warm-up after loading the model is not counted.

## What is new in version 12

- **Versions of an answer.** When you edit a question or press **Answer again**, the old answer is kept. Small arrows (‹ 2/3 ›) let you go back and forth between the versions. Up to 12 versions are kept for each message. Chats from before this version are not changed.
- **Voting on hard questions.** Settings → Advanced → "Three tries for hard questions". In Deep mode, for puzzles and math questions with a short answer (a number, a choice, yes or no), Lumen answers three times and shows the answer that most tries agree on. If all three differ it says it is not sure, and the confidence label drops to Low. It takes two to three times as long, so it is off until you turn it on.
- **Prompt versions and example answers.** Settings → Advanced → "Base instructions" has three versions of the base instructions (v1, v2, v3). Each changes one thing, and you can switch at any time. Settings → Advanced → "Example answers" holds twenty model answers (explain, compare, how-to, both sides, "I don't know"). After you approve or rewrite at least three, Lumen shows the AI the two or three most similar ones before your question (this adds up to 600 tokens). A notice appears if an answer repeats 8 or more words in a row from an example.
- **Test lab.** Type `/lab` (or Settings → Advanced → Test lab). It gives numbers instead of guesses: a **quick check** after every change, **compare models** (0.8B, 2B, 4B), **prompt and example answers A/B**, a **voting test**, and a **speed** test. Every test runs your real prompt at temperature 0 with a fixed seed, in Fast mode and with no look-ups, so two runs can be compared. Answers are scored by code (fact found, math answer right, layout kept, simple words), never by another AI. A change passes if no category gets worse by one question and the speed does not fall by more than 20%. A run survives a page reload, and if the graphics memory runs out the test says so and carries on. **Copy the report** gives a Markdown summary.
- **Dictation (speak instead of type).** Settings → Voice → Dictation. Off by default. In Chrome it works in one of two ways, and Lumen tells you which one before you turn it on: **private** (the browser turns speech into text on your computer; it needs the language pack, which the browser can download once) or **online** (the browser sends your voice to its speech service, in Chrome that is Google; this needs a clear yes). A microphone button appears next to Send. Your words appear in the message box so you can fix them, and nothing is sent until you press Send. A line above the box says where your voice goes while it listens. **Esc** stops it.
- **Offline Wikipedia.** Settings → Look-ups → Offline Wikipedia. A copy of Wikipedia's articles, kept on your computer and searched there, so many questions need no internet and no look-up leaves your computer. Lumen never downloads Wikipedia by itself. You make one file once, on your Mac:
  1. Open **Terminal** and go to the `tools` folder that came with Lumen, for example `cd ~/Downloads/lumen/tools`.
  2. Type `python3 build_wiki_pack.py --download simple` and press Enter. It downloads Simple English Wikipedia (about 350 MB, one time) and writes `lumen-wiki-pack.jsonl.gz`. It needs nothing installed and takes about 10 minutes. You can check the script first with `python3 build_wiki_pack.py --selftest` (no internet needed).
  3. In Lumen press **Choose the pack file** and pick that file. The import takes a few minutes. Keep the page open; **Stop the import** keeps nothing.

  For a bigger copy from the full English Wikipedia (a 23 GB download and several hours of work for your Mac) use `python3 build_wiki_pack.py --download en --max-articles 100000`, which keeps the longest 100,000 articles. The pack holds the title and the opening part of each article as plain words. It has no pictures, tables or lists, and no redirects, disambiguation pages, lists or very short stubs.
  
  **How it is used.** Setting "First" (the default): Lumen looks in the copy first. If it finds an article whose title shares a key word with your question, it uses it and asks nobody. If not, or if the question is about something recent ("latest", "news", this year), it asks Wikipedia online as before; with no internet the copy fills in. "Only it": Wikipedia is never asked online. "Off": the copy stays but is not used. The AI is told the copy's date and to say so for things that change. The source shows as "Wikipedia (offline copy)" with the CC BY-SA 4.0 credit, saved chats and exports credit Wikipedia, and a notice appears if an answer copies 8 or more words in a row from the copy.

## What came with version 11 and earlier

The look is the calm, cream-and-teal design again. Under it, Lumen is much smarter and faster:

- **Auto mode** (the default). Lumen decides how much to think. "Hi" gets an instant answer, "why is the sky blue?" gets a short think, and "write a detailed report" gets Deep. You can still pick Fast, Think or Deep yourself. The answer says which one was used.
- **Bigger memory.** The AI keeps about 16,000 tokens in mind (roughly 12,000 words). You can change this in Settings → Model → Working memory. If your graphics chip cannot hold it, Lumen quietly uses a smaller size. In long chats, older messages are squeezed into a short summary, so the chat never "forgets" suddenly and never slows down.
- **Exact answers, not guesses.** Arithmetic, percentages, unit conversion, dates and "what time is it in Tokyo" are worked out by code on your computer.
- **Live data.** Weather and a 3-day forecast (Open-Meteo), currency rates (European Central Bank), crypto prices (CoinGecko).
- **Up-to-date facts.** Wikipedia (plus Simple English Wikipedia, Wiktionary and Wikivoyage), Wikipedia's news pages, Wikidata (exact facts like film directors, capitals, birth dates), news headlines from around the world (GDELT), tech news (Hacker News), and research papers (OpenAlex). They all run at the same time, so a look-up adds almost no waiting. Answers show numbered sources.
- **Optional web search.** Add a free key from Jina or Tavily in Settings and Lumen can search the whole web. Without a key it uses the sources above.
- **Your files.** Press **+** or drag a file into the chat: PDF, Word, PowerPoint, Excel, text, CSV, code. Long files are read in parts; for "summarize the whole file" Lumen first summarizes each part, then combines them. Paste a long text and it becomes a file automatically.
- **Web pages.** Paste a link and Lumen asks before reading it (see the privacy table).
- **Study tools.** `/quiz`, `/cards` and `/slides` make a quiz you can click through, flashcards you can flip, or a slide deck you can present full screen, download as one web page, or print as PDF. They work from a topic, your files, or the last answer.
- **Code tools.** Code blocks have Download. HTML and SVG can be previewed, JavaScript can be run, and Mermaid diagrams are drawn. All of it runs in a locked box with no internet and no access to your chats. JavaScript stops after 5 seconds. (Python cannot be run, only downloaded.)
- **Memory and rules.** Say "remember that my exam is on Friday" or use `/remember` (Lumen also notices facts by itself, see above). Settings → Memory → "Your rules" holds how Lumen should always answer. Everything stays on this computer.
- **Message menu.** Under each answer: copy, read aloud, answer again (think harder, faster, search first, without searching), see exactly what Lumen read for the answer, make a quiz or slides from it, save or print.
- **Commands.** Type `/` to see them: `/search`, `/deep`, `/fast`, `/think`, `/simple`, `/short`, `/tutor`, `/solve`, `/both`, `/essay`, `/review`, `/role`, `/quiz`, `/cards`, `/slides`, `/summarize`, `/weather`, `/convert`, `/time`, `/remember`, `/forget`, `/learned`, `/new`, `/export`, `/settings`, `/help`.
- **Search everything:** press **Cmd+K** (or Ctrl+K) to search your chats and find any action.
- **Backup:** Settings → Data and privacy → "Save a backup" saves your chats and memory as one file. "Restore a backup" brings them back. The backup never contains your web search key.

## What leaves your computer

Your questions are answered on your own graphics chip. Only these short messages go out, and only when needed:

| Service | What is sent |
|---|---|
| Wikipedia (and Wiktionary, Wikivoyage) | Your search words |
| Wikipedia news | Nothing about your question |
| Offline Wikipedia copy | Nothing. It is searched on your computer, and Lumen never downloads it |
| GitHub Pages, Hugging Face | Nothing from you but your internet address, like any web site: GitHub hosts the page, the AI engine and the page libraries (the folder `vendor/`), Hugging Face and GitHub deliver the model files when you press Download |
| Dictation (only if you turn it on) | Nothing from Lumen. Private mode keeps your voice on your computer. Online mode: the browser sends your voice to its speech service (in Chrome, Google) |
| Wikidata | The name of the article it found |
| GDELT news | Your search words (news questions only) |
| Hacker News | Your search words (tech news only) |
| OpenAlex | Your search words (research questions only) |
| Open-Meteo | The place name |
| Frankfurter | The currency codes |
| CoinGecko | The coin name |
| Jina Reader | The address of a page, only after you say yes |
| Web search (Jina or Tavily) | Your search words, only if you added a key |
| Daily reading | Wikipedia's news feed (nothing about you). If you allow it, also a few of your favorite topic words, the search words of questions that found nothing, and the titles of older notes to Wikipedia, GDELT and Hacker News. Words from your personal facts (your devices, name, city) and sensitive topics are never used. |

Never sent: your chats, files, memory, saved notes, name, rules or email address. (Your saved city is used only for weather questions where you did not name a place, and then only the place name goes to Open-Meteo.) Text found on the internet is treated as reference text only; Lumen never follows instructions that appear inside it. You can switch every source off in Settings → Look-ups, and "Check connections" tests them all.

## If something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| "This browser can't run the AI model" | Use Chrome 113+, or update to macOS 26 for Safari. |
| "Graphics chip ran out of memory" | Close other tabs and apps, choose a smaller Memory size, or pick a smaller model in Settings. |
| The download stopped | Check the internet and click Try again. Finished parts are kept. |
| The model sits at 0% or will not start | Lumen now notices this by itself. If nothing happens for a minute it restarts the loading once, then tells you what it found. The usual causes: **Lumen is open in another tab or window** (close the other one and reload), a **network that blocks the model server** (some school and work networks do; try your phone hotspot), or **not enough memory** (close other tabs and apps). If it still fails, press "Delete the saved model and start over". |
| The model downloads again every time | Don't use a private window. Chrome may also clear site data when the disk is nearly full. |
| It said a file was broken and downloaded the model again and again | Fixed in version 18. Lumen now checks every file and downloads only a broken one again. Settings → Model → **Check the saved files** does it by hand. If the browser keeps damaging files, Lumen saves the model another way by itself. |
| "There is not enough free storage for this model" | Free some disk space, close private windows, delete a model you no longer use (Settings → Model), or pick a smaller model. Nothing was downloaded. |
| I don't know which model fits my computer | Profile menu (bottom left) → **Set up Lumen for this computer**, or the model button in the message box → **Which model fits my computer?** |
| The page restarted while the AI was thinking | Lumen now says why (tab put to sleep, background tab, or a crash from graphics memory). Close big tabs and apps, keep Lumen in front, and in Chrome add the site under Settings → Performance → "Always keep these sites active". Press Try again. |
| A source shows an orange or red dot | Open Settings → Look-ups → Check connections. A source that fails three times is paused for a few minutes so it never slows an answer. |
| Answers feel slow | Use Auto or Fast, close other tabs, or choose the 0.8B model. |

## Honest limits

- Small models are much weaker than big cloud AIs. Lumen can still be wrong, especially about exact numbers, dates and hard math. Check the sources under each answer. Do not use it for medical, legal or exam-critical answers.
- The AI's own knowledge stops around the end of 2025. For anything newer it needs a source; if it finds none it says so.
- News headlines give the title, source and date, not the whole article.
- There are no live stock prices (there is no free, key-free source that works in a browser).
- The websites above can change or block requests. Lumen was tested with pretend versions of them, so if one of them behaves differently in real life, that source just shows a red dot and the others keep working. The Tavily and Jina web search options are the most likely to need changes.
- Mermaid diagrams and the PDF reader are loaded from the internet the first time you use them.
- The built-in facts come from a research round of about 20 searches and a few pages, not from 1,000 websites, and the list is short (about 310 facts). It is a good base, not an encyclopedia. Facts about people in office, results and news will become old; the date "checked 11 October 2026" is shown to the AI. Some things were left out on purpose because they could not be confirmed (for example who runs Venezuela today). You can read every fact in the file `core-facts.txt` that comes with Lumen. To refresh them, ask Claude for a new research round.
- Check facts only knows the built-in facts and Wikipedia. A claim about something they do not cover will say "Could not confirm". Wikipedia search wants all the words to match, so Lumen retries without numbers and with just the names, but it can still miss a page.
- The both-sides length check counts characters, not quality. It cannot tell if a small model describes one side in a weaker way.
- Essay feedback is only as good as a 2B-parameter model reading your paragraph. The quote check proves a comment is about your real sentence; it does not prove the comment is right. Treat it as a second pair of eyes, not a teacher. It was tested with a pretend model, so it has not been scored on essays with planted mistakes yet.
- The solver's calculator check only covers simple steps written as numbers with one sign, like `150 / 2 = 75`. It cannot check steps with letters (`3x = 15`), word problems in sentences, or a wrong method. So "all correct" means the sums are right, not that the whole solution is right. A small model can also ignore the six-part layout; Lumen asks once and then tells you what is missing. Roles are only instructions: a small model follows them most of the time, not always.
- The source check looks at details, not meaning. It finds a year, a number or a name that no source contains. It cannot tell that a sentence is wrong when all its details are in the notes, and it misses a real name used in the wrong place. Numbers under 100 are not checked, and a correct detail that the AI knew but the notes did not contain is also reported (and may be taken out). Lumen was tested with a pretend AI model; how much it helps with the real model is something the Usage page will show you over a few days. I could not measure it myself.
- The 9B model and Max mode are slower still (about half the speed of the 4B) and need about 6.4 GB of graphics memory. They were added without a real graphics chip to test on, so the real run on a strong computer is the true test. If the page stops, Lumen explains what probably happened, but it cannot always know the real cause.
- The bigger model (4B) is slower and needs about 3.9 GB of graphics memory. On a Mac with 8 GB, close other tabs and apps first.
- The AI model itself does not change. "Learning" means saved notes and habits, not a smarter brain, so a very small model can still misunderstand a note. If it gets something wrong, tell it with "remember that …" and check the sources.
- "The most-known meaning" is a good guess, not a promise. It uses Wikipedia's page views, so a name that is suddenly in the news can win over an older, more famous meaning. That is why the other meanings are always one tap away. If Wikipedia's page-view data cannot be reached, Lumen falls back to the normal search order and still shows the other meanings.
- The daily reading only runs while the Lumen page is open in your browser (a web page cannot wake itself up while closed). If you open Lumen every day, it keeps up; if you do not, press **Learn now**.
- Lumen picks facts out of your messages with simple rules, not with the AI model, so it can miss a fact or (rarely) save one you did not mean. Check Settings → Memory → "What Lumen remembers" now and then; every fact has a delete button.
- Dictation uses a browser feature that is still new. Private dictation works only if your Chrome offers it for your language; if it does not, Lumen says so and the only way is online dictation, where the browser (not Lumen) sends your voice to Google. It was tested with a pretend speech engine, not with a real microphone.
- Voting only helps for short answers that can be compared, and it takes two to three times as long. It was tested with a pretend model that is wrong on purpose, so whether it makes the real 2B model more accurate is something only the Test lab's voting test can tell you on your Mac.
- The Test lab scores with simple code rules (a key word is there, the number is right, the layout is kept). It is a guide, not a grade: 50 questions are a small sample, and a difference of one question can be luck, so repeat a run before you decide. Speed numbers depend on what else your Mac is doing. The questions and the scoring were tested with a pretend model; the real numbers come from your own Mac.
- Offline Wikipedia finds articles by their words (with a small fix for typing mistakes), not by meaning. A question that uses other words than the article may find nothing; in "First" mode Lumen then asks Wikipedia online, in "Only it" mode you get no Wikipedia notes. The pack is a snapshot: the date shown is the day you built the file. Importing takes minutes (in a test, 60,000 articles took about two and a half minutes) and the copy uses roughly four times the size of the pack file on disk (a 19 MB file used about 68 MB), plus 80 to 120 MB of extra memory while importing; afterwards it needs almost no memory. Removing the copy deletes it at once, but the browser may take a while to give the disk space back. The builder script and the importer were tested on a made-up Wikipedia file in the real format and on made-up packs of up to 60,000 articles, not on the real Wikipedia download, which cannot be reached from where Lumen was built. If Wikipedia's real file behaves differently, the script's "--selftest" and the error messages in Lumen say what went wrong.

- The legal pages were written from a checklist, not by a lawyer. They say what Lumen really does today, but if Lumen changes (a new look-up source, analytics, accounts) they must change too. The checklist itself lists questions for a Slovenian lawyer, for example whether a free hobby site needs a full postal address, and how the AI Act's marking rule works for plain text. Since version 16 the AI engine and the page libraries are in the folder `vendor/` and no public content network is used. I tested that with the real files in a real browser (the engine file loads, also in the background worker, and knows the Qwen3.5 models), but without a graphics chip I could not run a real model here, so the first start on your Mac is the real test. The name "Lumen" is also used by other companies (one of them a telecom company with an AI helper called "Ask Lumen"); that is low risk for a free hobby project, but rename it before you earn money or publish an app.
- The help notice for self-harm uses a list of phrases. It is a safety net, not a safe system.
- Version 18's new downloader was tested with the real WebLLM storage code, real browser storage and a pretend model server, including cut connections, empty saved files, a full disk, a server without byte ranges and a browser that damages files. The real Qwen3.5 9B files could not be downloaded where Lumen was built (Hugging Face could not be reached from there), so the first real download on your Mac is the final test. If a file still cannot be saved, Lumen now says exactly what happened instead of starting over.
- The setup's model advice is a rule of thumb from the memory and chip you choose. A browser cannot see how much memory a computer has, and other open apps use memory too, so a model marked "fits" can still be too big on a busy computer. Lumen then says so and offers a smaller one.
- The look follows Claude's style, but Lumen is a separate hobby project. It is not made by, connected to or endorsed by Anthropic, and it uses its own name and mark.

Want smarter answers? Open Settings and switch to the 4B model (2.4 GB), or on a strong computer (16 GB of RAM) to the 9B model (5.1 GB). It reasons better, but is about half as fast and needs more memory, so close other tabs first.
